Architecture

EpiChat is a web app with a conversational agent and a private simulation service. The model handles language; tools write the configuration, apply the data and run the simulation, so a fetched number or a literature estimate is never retyped by the model. 

Browser ──SSE──▶ web app (Next.js on Vercel)
                   │  agent loop: Claude (Opus 5.5 by default) · nine tools · web search and fetch
                   │  Supabase: sign-in · consent · conversations · runs · events · feedback
                   │  public data: UN Population Data Portal · WHO GHO · World Bank Data360
                   ▼
            sim service (FastAPI · Python 3.12 · private, reached only by the web app)
                   │  validate (schema v2) → build the Starsim 3.7.2 model → run in a child process
                   │  up to 5 replicates · up to 2 repairs · diagnostics · Word and PDF export
                   ▼
            series · statistics · diagnostics → cards, charts and "Try next" replies

The web agent replaced the v0.3 template pipeline for the pilot. The Python package, its command-line interface and the Streamlit prototype remain in the repository.

Agent tools

Nine tools, plus the model provider's web search and web fetch. Each result appears in the conversation as a card.

ToolWhat it does
lookup_diseaseReads a disease's cited parameters, each with a status: a usable consensus, estimates without an agreed value, held back under review, or nothing yet.
configure_simulationCreates or edits the configuration, passing only what changed: the model, the R₀ (β is calibrated to it), durations, fatality, seeding, interventions, a fit, a chosen reference, and any other parameter by its registry path. Everything is validated at once; an error names the parameter.
describe_parametersLists every parameter of the current model and network with its value, bounds, unit, source and literature range.
fetch_demographicsUN World Population Prospects for a country: the age pyramid (switching to the age-structured network), birth and death rates, the population for scaling, the contact matrix and the household size. The R₀ is held where it was when the new network can carry it.
fetch_vaccination_coverageWHO routine coverage for a disease and country, applied as immunity at the start, or as a day-0 campaign when the database has the vaccine's efficacy.
fetch_health_systemWorld Bank hospital beds, physicians, nurses and UHC coverage; sets a treatment's daily capacity from the beds.
run_simulationRuns the configuration and returns the series, the statistics (scaled to the real population when demographics were fetched) and the diagnostics.
write_reportWrites the report's summary, meaning, limitations and next steps; its tables and figures come from the stored results.
rememberKeeps a one-line note about the participant (a role, a situation, a preference) for later conversations.

Disease database

A literature-backed database (epichat/data/disease_parameters.json) covers 16 diseases. For each parameter it keeps a consensus value with its range and the individual published estimates, each with its title, year and link. The agent reads a status with every value and is told never to quote a number the database holds back. In the app's Data section every single-number estimate has a "Use this value" button; the tool then reads the value and its citation from the reference data, so neither can be mistyped, and the value carries the paper's title for as long as it stands.

Networks

Random

Fresh contacts every day, a constant number or Poisson-distributed, optionally lasting several days. The R₀ formula is exact when contacts do not last.

Age-structured

Three age groups (under 18, 18 to 64, 65 and over) mixing by a 3×3 contact matrix: POLYMOD's European defaults, or the country's Prem et al. 2021 matrix once its demographics are fetched (177 countries). The R₀ formula is exact. The measured R₀ is not available on this network, because Starsim's mixing pools do not record who infected whom.

Hybrid: households, schools, workplaces and community

Four layers as in Covasim, with the country's mean household size (UN, 189 countries) and the layers' sizes and transmissibilities as parameters. The R₀ formula is approximate here, so a short pilot run, in which only the seeds transmit, measures it and adjusts β.

At the same R₀ the final size is close on every network: the networks differ in who meets whom and in what the interventions act on.

Interventions

Six types, combined freely, named in the configuration vaccine, treatment, seasonality, contact_reduction, transmission_reduction and behavior_response. Every effect size is an illustrative assumption unless the database carries it, and the app labels it so.

Vaccine

Routine (a yearly probability for each eligible person) or a campaign (the coverage reached evenly over a window), with an efficacy, leaky or all-or-nothing protection, an age range and a protection duration. Coverage fetched from WHO becomes immunity at the start, or a day-0 campaign when the database has the vaccine's efficacy.

Treatment

A share of infectious people treated each day, after a delay, with an efficacy and an optional daily capacity, which the health-system data sets from the country's hospital beds. In SEIAR it can treat symptomatic cases only.

Seasonality

A yearly cycle in transmission: scale is its size (0.2 moves transmission between 80% and 120% of β) and shift moves the peak through the year.

Contact reduction

One layer's contacts scaled over a window. A school closure is the school layer of the hybrid network at scale 1; distancing scales every layer. The random and age-structured networks take every layer only.

Transmission reduction

Every contact's transmissibility scaled over a window, such as masks.

Behaviour response

Transmission falls as the share of people infectious rises, averaged over the last week by default, and recovers as it falls: people being careful, not a measure. It flattens the peak, but because it relaxes it cannot keep the total far below the herd immunity threshold in a long run.

Fit · limits on spread

A run can be fitted to one outcome you know: the attack rate, the peak day or the peak size. The service searches the transmission rate (or the network multiplier) in at most eight runs and reports what it reached. To match a known attack rate while the R₀ stays where it was set, the fit can move the variation in susceptibility or the behaviour response instead.

Without an intervention an epidemic reaches the share the final-size equation gives for its R₀, on every network (89% at R₀ 2.5): only the depletion of susceptibles stops it. Two mechanisms limit spread without a dated measure, both off by default and both named by the agent as assumptions when it offers them: variation in susceptibility (the most susceptible are infected first, so at a coefficient of variation of 1 the epidemic ends near 60%) and the behaviour response.

Diagnostics

After every run the service reads the full series and returns diagnostics, each with levers: a set of parameter values, kept within the registry's bounds and the literature range, or intervention entries. The agent offers at most three as suggested replies, a warning's fixes before any intervention, applies the one you choose, reruns, and says what changed.

DiagnosticFires when
early_fadeoutThe seeding infected nobody, or the outbreak died out early: fewer infections than twice the seeds, or under 1% of the agents when nothing held transmission down from day 0
instant_peakThe peak is the seeding itself
unfinishedThe run ended before the epidemic did
cfr_out_of_rangeThe case fatality ratio is outside the literature range
final_size_gapThe attack rate does not match the configured R₀
sharp_curveEvery period is the same for everyone, which sharpens the curve
fatality_on_all_infectionsThe fatality ratio applies to every infection, asymptomatic ones included
no_reinfectionImmunity outlasts the run, so waning cannot be seen
high_varianceThe replicates disagree widely
well_mixedNearly everyone was infected; it offers the two limits on spread
r0_gapThe measured R₀ is far from the configured one
what_ifThe run had an epidemic: up to four interventions to try (a vaccination campaign, a school closure, distancing, masks, a behaviour response), each starting the day 1% of the agents had been infected, leaving out what the scenario already has

Outputs

  • Charts. Four views of every run: compartments, new infections, cumulative infections and deaths. With replicates, the median run and a 10–90% band.
  • The details panel. Scenario, Data (the fetched values with citations and the literature reference list), Parameters (every value with its source), Runs, Report, Activity and Profile.
  • Reports. The agent's summary, meaning, limitations and next steps; tables and figures from the stored runs; every data source. Download as Word, PDF, HTML or Markdown.
  • Share links. A read-only copy of the conversation at that moment, without your email.
  • Memory. One-line notes about you (a role, a situation, a preference) that carry into later conversations; you can see and edit them in your profile.
  • Feedback. A thumbs up or down on any reply.

Privacy · the study

During the pilot EpiChat is open to study participants with an Emory email address, who sign in with an emailed code. Using it is taking part in the study: at first sign-in a consent screen says what is recorded, among other things your email address and the kind of participant you are, your conversations, the agent's summarized reasoning, how you use the app, your browser and device type, your profile answers and what the agent remembers about you, and how often a link you share is opened, and it asks you to agree. Your messages go to Anthropic's Claude API to produce replies; simulation settings go to the project's own simulation service. The data is kept for the duration of the study on servers in the United States, and deleting a conversation hides it from you but keeps it for the study. Each participant has a daily turn limit, and the project has a monthly spending cap.

Risks · mitigations

RiskMitigation
Invalid simulation codeThe model never writes code; tools pass structured values through one registry and schema
Parameter hallucinationValues come from you, the database, fetched data or a chosen reference, each tagged with its source; out-of-range values are flagged; illustrative assumptions are labelled; held-back values are never quoted
Wrong R₀ on a structured networkβ calibrated exactly on the random and age-structured networks; on the hybrid network a pilot run measures it and adjusts β
Implausible resultsDiagnostics on every run, each with suggested fixes
Execution failureUp to two automatic repairs of the configuration, with a visible repair log
Runaway runs and costA 120-second run limit, a cap of 500,000 agent-years per run, a daily turn limit per participant and a monthly budget
API instabilityStarsim pinned at 3.7.2; a fixed simulation baseline in the test suite
